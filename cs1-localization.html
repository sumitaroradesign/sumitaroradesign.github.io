01

What this product is, and why it matters

The platform is an internal localisation system used across every IKEA market to manage the translation and adaptation of content at scale. It sits at the centre of a complex content supply chain — deeply integrated with both the Product Information Management (PIM) and Digital Asset Management (DAM) systems.

Every piece of customer-facing content that IKEA produces — product specifications, marketing copy, store communications, labels — passes through this system before it reaches end customers in the market.

Who uses it

  • Translators and localisation specialists
  • Content editors across markets
  • Legal reviewers and proofreaders
  • Retail teams managing local content

What gets localised

  • Product information and specifications
  • Marketing and campaign content
  • Store communication materials
  • Labels and customer-facing text
My role

Leading design across the full lifecycle

I led the end-to-end UX design for this platform while contributing to product strategy. My work spanned user research, journey mapping, system architecture decisions, and close collaboration with regional teams, engineers, and business stakeholders across multiple markets.

This wasn't a single project with a clear end date — it was an ongoing modernisation effort, built iteratively alongside users and released market by market until full global rollout.

02

What was broken

As part of a broader legacy modernisation programme, a new platform was needed to support IKEA's expansion and an ever-growing range of content types. The old solution had reached its limits in almost every dimension.

Problem 01
No visibility into what matters
Users had no overview or shared backlog. They clicked around guessing what was new or urgent, and often defaulted to what felt familiar — not what was highest priority for the business.
Problem 02
Fully manual, nothing reusable
Translators had to click into every single piece of text individually. Content could not be reused across formats, forcing manual copy-paste and creating inconsistency at scale.
Problem 03
Fragmented metadata and constant context-switching
Critical information lived in separate screens or external tools. Users had to leave the platform to get what they needed, breaking flow and reducing the chance they'd check important context at all.
Problem 04
No traceability of changes
There was no audit trail for translation changes, which created serious compliance risks and made it impossible to confirm product warranty accuracy across markets.
Problem 05
Search that worked against users
Search required users to know the text category upfront — a system logic that didn't match how people actually think. The result was hit-and-trial searching and results that were slow to parse.
Problem 06
Workflow states too limited to support real work
Legal reviews and proofreading happened outside the system entirely — in downloaded Excel files. Collaboration was a workaround, not a feature.
03

How I understood the problem

I began with an investigative evaluation study — a structured analysis of the legacy system's actual usage patterns. This was followed by a survey sent to understand which parts of the flow had the most friction, who was using the application, and what they did most often.

Survey responses and previously collected pain points were then mapped into twelve distinct problem areas — a clear, prioritised picture of where and why the system was failing.

04

Insight to solution

With the problems mapped, I ran a competitive assessment of comparable tools on the market to evaluate whether we should build or buy, and identified the gaps between IKEA's technical capabilities and what was currently on offer. That gap analysis shaped the platform's architecture and data model — defining what to build natively, what to integrate, and how content had to be structured to scale across markets. Rather than a single big-bang launch, I worked iteratively — building, testing, and validating with a representative set of markets, so each release was grounded in evidence before it scaled.

Prioritised problems were addressed through a paired insight and solution — moving from what users actually experienced to what the redesigned system needed to provide.

Insight
Users didn't lack capability — they lacked clarity on what to work on next
Without a shared overview, effort drifted towards familiar tasks rather than business-critical ones.
Solution — Centralised task overview
  • Clear visibility of pending, in-progress, and completed work
  • Priority signals surfaced at the top of every queue
  • Reduced reliance on individual judgment for prioritisation
Insight
Repetitive manual tasks had become normalised
Users had adapted to the friction rather than questioning it. Content could not be reused across formats, forcing manual duplication every time.
Solution — Reusable content structures
  • Content reusable across content types and formats
  • Eliminated copy-paste workflows
  • Improved consistency and reduced errors at scale
Insight
Context-switching was breaking concentration and decisions
Metadata was spread across separate screens and external systems. Users left the platform to get what they needed — and often didn't return.
Solution — Unified contextual metadata
  • Relevant data surfaced within the workflow, not behind clicks
  • Removed the need to switch between tools mid-task
  • Improved decision-making speed and confidence
Insight
Search relied on system logic rather than user mental models
Requiring a text category upfront was how the database worked — not how translators thought. The mismatch meant hit-and-trial searching as standard practice.
Solution — Redesigned search and discovery
  • Reduced dependency on rigid, system-first filters
  • Natural language and content-based discovery
  • Significantly faster access to relevant content
Insight
Collaboration was happening around the system, not inside it
Legal reviews and proofreading were handled in downloaded Excel files. The system had too few workflow states to support what teams actually needed to do.
Solution — Expanded in-system workflow
  • Legal and proofreading workflows fully supported within the platform
  • Eliminated Excel-based workarounds
  • Real-time collaboration replacing offline coordination
Insight
Slow and unstable systems erode trust — and adoption
Performance issues weren't just frustrating — they had changed user behaviour. People avoided features because they expected the system to fail.
Solution — Engineering partnership on performance
  • Worked directly with engineering to prioritize performance
  • Rebuilt user confidence through stability
05

Measured impact

The platform rolled out across IKEA's markets, with adoption growing as new capabilities shipped. The 63 markets map to roughly 135 country–language combinations: about 47 rely on a local translator, while the rest inherit translations from another market. Impact was measured through usability testing, analytics, and time-on-task and SLA tracking before and after rollout — most directly for the markets translating locally.

06

What I took away

Learning 01
Workarounds are data
When users download Excel files to do legal reviews, that's not a quirk — it's a design failure made visible. The workaround is the signal. Every informal process deserves a formal question: why does this exist?
Learning 02
Buy vs build is a UX decision too
Recommending the right architecture — rather than assuming we'd build everything — was one of the highest-impact design decisions I made. Understanding technical options is part of the UX brief.
Learning 03
Global scale demands radical simplicity
With 63 markets and many languages, any ambiguity in the interface multiplied into support requests, errors, and delays. Clarity at the component level had outsized downstream impact.
Learning 04
Performance is a design requirement
Users had adapted their behaviour around a slow system — avoiding certain features entirely. Shipping a faster platform changed not just experience but what was possible. Speed is a feature.
